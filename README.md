# HDB Financial Services IPO: Evaluation and Pricing Critique

**Was HDB Financial's ₹12,500 crore IPO priced right, and what should the bankers have done differently?**

## The answer

It was priced at the cap when it should have been priced at the floor. The ₹740 issue price was about 8.4% above a peer-average P/E benchmark of about ₹682, and the multiple was set on trough earnings. FY25 profit had fallen 11.6% and ROE had compressed to 14.7%, below every premium comparable the deal was benchmarked against, because credit costs had nearly doubled.

The risk the prospectus disclosed then arrived on schedule. Within two quarters of listing, gross NPAs rose to 2.81%, provisions jumped about 74% year on year and the stock traded below its issue price. The prospectus risk factors worked as leading indicators, not boilerplate.

## Recommendation

Anchor the price at or near the ₹700 floor (about 25.5x FY25 EPS) rather than the ₹740 cap (about 27.1x). That would have left a valuation cushion for the credit-cost normalisation the company itself disclosed, and better matched the subscription book, which signalled that retail demand would not follow institutional demand.

## Approach

1. Read the red herring prospectus directly rather than secondary summaries.
2. Rebuilt weighted average EPS (₹28.20, matching the prospectus) and benchmarked it against the eight listed peers the prospectus names.
3. Tracked subscription by investor category, the listing day, 3 and 6 month performance, and the share price around each anchor lock-in expiry.
4. Tested whether disclosed risks crystallised after listing.
